Chuong Nguyen

B.S. in Computer Science, 2026 · UC San Diego

I am a recent Computer Science graduate and a research assistant at the Halıcıoğlu Data Science Institute. I study how AI agents can move from answering questions to actively expanding their understanding of the world: how they represent what they know, recognize what remains unexplained, decide which connections are worth investigating, and validate which discoveries deserve to change what they believe. My work spans knowledge representation, evaluation of LLM-generated interpretation, and agentic deep research systems.

Research

I build AI agents that move from answering questions to expanding understanding, aligned with human interpretation. My interests span knowledge discovery and representation, curiosity-driven and abductive investigation, validating LLM interpretations against human judgment, agent systems design, and resource-aware reinforcement learning.

Knowledge Representation

Turning unstructured evidence into structured, relational knowledge an agent can reason over, without fixing that structure so tightly that unexpected relationships become impossible to discover (LOGOS).

Evaluation & Validation

Asking what an AI system has actually understood, and how to tell a genuinely supported conclusion from a merely plausible one (QualAlign, COLM 2026; Position Paper, ICML 2026).

Agentic Research & Curiosity

Building tool-using agents that research questions and synthesize evidence (Episteme), and exploring how agents could decide which question to pursue next.

Technology & Society

Prior work on parental concerns in online gaming platforms, using large-scale social media analysis and user studies to inform safer, more transparent platform design (Roblox Safety, IEEE ISTAS 2024).

LOGOS

Co-developed and led implementation of an LLM-driven framework that turns unstructured evidence (interview transcripts, legal texts, agent trajectories) into hierarchical schemas, building semantic clustering, relation-aware graph reasoning, and iterative refinement. Reaches an 88.2% matching rate against expert-developed schemas.

QualAlign (COLM 2026)

Co-built a benchmark for automated qualitative coding and ran the baselines. Organized evaluation metrics into lexical, semantic, and model-based interpretive categories, showing that metrics agree within a category but diverge across them.

Position Paper (ICML 2026)

Co-defined a framework that crosses levels of meaning-making with levels of modeling, and mapped existing automated qualitative-analysis systems onto it.
